Abstract

Dynamic manipulation requires robots to infer target motion and respond promptly, yet existing World–Action Models (WAMs) typically condition only on the current frame and execute large backbones synchronously, limiting motion awareness and responsive control in dynamic scenes. We propose DynamicWAM, a compact WAM for dynamic object manipulation with dual-path motion conditioning. DynamicWAM introduces history-flow conditioning, encoding temporally aligned optical-flow maps alongside the current observation through an unchanged pretrained video VAE to preserve spatial motion structure, while injecting numerical descriptors of displacement, duration, velocity, and acceleration into the action expert to provide metric scale and temporal dynamics.

The two complementary paths are fused through joint world–action attention. A distilled compact backbone and real-time chunking (RTC)-based asynchronous execution further enable responsive control. On DOMINO, DynamicWAM achieves a 38.2% success rate and a 53.2 manipulation score, outperforming all evaluated baselines. Across 12 real-world tasks spanning linear, circular, and complex target motion, it achieves a 46.7% average success rate, exceeding the strongest baseline by 22.9 percentage points. These results demonstrate that dual-path motion conditioning effectively enables motion-aware control of moving targets.

The problem

The current frame is not enough — and normalized flow is not calibrated

A current-frame world–action model conditions on the current composite observation, the language instruction, and the robot state. In a dynamic scene this condition is ambiguous by construction: the same image and the same joint readings are compatible with a target that is creeping and a target that is sprinting. Whatever the policy does next, it is guessing the one variable that decides interception — velocity.

Adding rendered optical-flow history repairs observability, but not calibration. Each flow map is rendered with direction as hue and magnitude normalized by that map's own 99th percentile, so scaling the underlying flow field leaves the rendering unchanged:

ρ(αU) = ρ(U), α > 0

The image can say “the target is moving up-right, over here” — it cannot say how fast. DynamicWAM's answer is a division of labor, not generic multimodal fusion: flow images carry spatial motion structure, and a separate numeric path carries the magnitude and time variables the rendering discards.

Cbase = (Ot, ℓ, st) dynamically aliased
Cflow = Cbase + (R1…R4) observable, uncalibrated
Cfull = Cflow + (m1…m4) observable + calibrated

The 12 numbers the rendering can't keep

Each of the four intervals contributes one 12-D numerical descriptor, standardized with dataset-level statistics. Spatial quantities live on the 64×64 flow grid; time is exact simulator seconds. Invalid intervals get learned validity embeddings — missing evidence is never silently treated as zero motion.

Where the tokens matter most

The largest gains come from tasks that need accurately timed contact with a moving target — success along current-frame → + flow → full model:

move_pillbottle_pad 42 → 55 → 91% timed pick from a moving pad
beat_block_hammer 36 → 49 → 84% striking a moving block
place_phone_stand 33 → 32 → 80% flow alone doesn't help here — the numerical path does

The numerical-path comparison evaluates the complete path — motion tokens together with their associated training stages. And the gains are not uniform: the full model trails the flow-only variant on put_object_cabinet (−10) and place_can_basket (−8); complete per-task results are in the paper's appendix.

Results · real robot

46.7% across 12 real-world tasks — and the only scores on compound motion

Twelve tasks in three motion regimes: linear — an RC vehicle, an inclined rail, a conveyor belt, a self-propelled toy; uniform circular — objects on a rotating turntable; and nonlinear or compound — random bouncing, simultaneous translation and rotation, curved trajectories. Every method is fine-tuned on the same 1,200 demonstrations and evaluated for 20 trials per task on Franka Research 3 arms with parallel-jaw grippers. DynamicWAM deploys with Real-Time Chunking: the next action chunk is predicted while the current one executes.
